Rolling Stock Optimisation

A mixed-integer optimisation model for assigning rolling stock to a railway timetable while satisfying passenger demand, infrastructure constraints, and operational requirements.

StackJulia, JuMP, Gurobi RoleMaster's thesis at DSB StatusCompleted
```

What it does

The project develops an optimisation model for assigning rolling stock to the trains in a single weekday timetable. The objective is to find a feasible and cost-efficient allocation of train units while meeting passenger demand and respecting operational and infrastructure constraints.

The model determines the composition of each train and ensures that the required number of units of each type is available throughout the timetable. The resulting composition can then be translated into an operational schedule using individual rolling stock units.

How it's built

The optimisation problem is formulated as a mixed-integer linear program in Julia using JuMP and solved with Gurobi. Timetable, passenger, infrastructure, and rolling stock data are combined and preprocessed before being passed to the optimisation model.

Several model variants were developed to investigate different levels of operational detail. The baseline model focuses on the required rolling stock capacity, while additional constraints preserve unit types throughout train journeys and account for train movements connected through the same operational service.

Optimisation

A key challenge is balancing the mathematical model with the practical requirements of railway operations. Constraints cover passenger capacity, electrification, train compositions, unit availability, and the conservation of rolling stock between the beginning and end of the planning period.

The final model was evaluated using real DSB timetable data. Increasing the level of operational detail significantly increases the size and computational complexity of the optimisation problem, making the trade-off between model accuracy and solution time an important part of the project.

Results

The different model formulations were compared in terms of feasibility, rolling stock requirements, solution quality, and computational performance. The results show how incorporating operational constraints changes the resulting allocation and can require substantially larger optimisation models.

Thesis

Master's thesis conducted at the Technical University of Denmark (DTU) in collaboration with DSB.
```
