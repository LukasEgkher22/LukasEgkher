Prediction of ENSO Events using a STAR Model

A time series analysis of the El Niño–Southern Oscillation using linear autoregressive and nonlinear Smooth Transition Autoregressive (STAR) models.

StackR, Time Series Analysis, Statistics RoleBachelor's thesis StatusCompleted · 2023

Overview

This bachelor's thesis investigates whether nonlinear Smooth Transition Autoregressive (STAR) models can improve the prediction of El Niño– Southern Oscillation (ENSO) events compared with conventional linear autoregressive models.

The analysis uses the Southern Oscillation Index (SOI), a commonly used indicator of ENSO activity based on atmospheric pressure differences between Tahiti and Darwin.

Approach

A linear autoregressive model is first estimated and used as a baseline. Linearity is then tested using the methodology proposed for STAR models. After rejecting linearity, different smooth transition specifications are investigated, including Logistic STAR (LSTAR) and Exponential STAR (ESTAR) models.

The nonlinear models introduce a transition function that allows the dynamics of the time series to change smoothly between different regimes. Model parameters are estimated using a combination of nonlinear estimation and grid search.

Results

Both STAR models provide a better fit to the training data than the linear autoregressive model. The ESTAR model reduces the residual sum of squares by approximately 4.3%, while the LSTAR model achieves a reduction of approximately 5.1%.

However, this improvement does not transfer to the test data. Both nonlinear models perform worse than the linear model when evaluated on observations from 2001 to 2023, indicating that the additional flexibility of the STAR models leads to overfitting.

The results therefore suggest that, for this dataset, the more complex nonlinear models do not provide a meaningful improvement for forecasting ENSO events based solely on the Southern Oscillation Index.

Key Findings

  • Linearity of the SOI time series was rejected for a delay of three months.
  • An ESTAR model was selected based on the transition-function tests.
  • Both ESTAR and LSTAR models fit the training data better than the linear model.
  • The nonlinear models performed worse on the independent test data.
  • The results indicate overfitting and highlight the limitations of using the SOI alone for ENSO prediction.

Thesis

The complete bachelor's thesis contains the theoretical development of STAR models, the statistical testing procedure, the analysis of the Southern Oscillation Index, and the empirical comparison of the different forecasting models.
